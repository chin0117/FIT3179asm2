CHAPTER 1 / SPATIAL PATTERNS

Where is tourism activity concentrated?

These three different map idioms show state demand, visits to individual protected areas, and interstate corridors. The first and third maps are general travel context, not measured nature-based trips.

01 · State choropleth map

All-purpose domestic day-trip volume by destination state. Darker colours mean more trips, but do not mean a higher share of nature-based tourism.

Source: TRA Domestic Tourism Statistics (DoTS), June quarter 2026; state boundary GeoJSON. Units: thousands of trips.

02 · Proportional-symbol map

The most-visited NSW protected areas are clustered near Sydney and the Blue Mountains. Bubble size represents visits, not unique visitors; dot locations are approximate.

Source: NSW NPWS Park Visitation Survey as reported by Destination NSW, FY2024-25; top 10 only. Units: millions of visits.

03 · Two-way interstate flow map

Line thickness highlights major domestic overnight tourism corridors. Opposite directions are aggregated to avoid plotting two lines on precisely the same route.

Source: TRA NVS, year ending December 2024. All-purpose overnight tourism, not verified park-related trips. Straight connections represent state-pair movement, not roads or flight paths.

CHAPTER 2 / NATURE-BASED EVIDENCE

Nature participation grew, but not evenly

The 2008-2015 State of the Environment series is the nature-specific historical evidence. The 2024-25 parks sample adds a recent spatial snapshot, not a continuation of the same series.

04 · Streamgraph

Shows how the three nature visitor segments grew and how their relative sizes evolved. Centred stacking is designed for composition, not reading precise totals.

Source: State of the Environment 2016 / Tourism Research Australia, 2008-2015. Million visitor-segment observations.

07 · Slope chart

A more precise comparison of 2008 versus 2015 shows domestic day nature visitors increasing from 12.81m to 19.02m (+48.5%).

Source: State of the Environment 2016 nature visitors dataset.

09 · Waterfall chart

Breaks the increase in combined reported visitor-segment counts into domestic day, domestic overnight and international changes. This total must not be called unique people.

Calculated from State of the Environment 2016, 2008-2015. Every step is recomputed from the CSV, rather than manually entered.

11 · Lorenz concentration curve

Examines how evenly visits are distributed across the ten reported leading NSW parks. The dashed diagonal represents an exactly equal distribution.

Calculated from Destination NSW's ten most-visited park figures, FY2024-25. Does not describe the distribution across all NSW protected areas.

CHAPTER 3 / DOMESTIC TOURISM CONTEXT

How do wider travel patterns differ?

The following five visualisations describe broader domestic travel behaviour: quarterly volume, relative visitor nights, spending, trip duration, and main reason for travel. None directly measures nature-based participation.

05 · Small-multiple line charts

Quarterly overnight trips have different shapes across states. State-specific vertical scales make trends visible but should not be used to compare absolute heights.

Source: TRA DoTS, Jun 2025 to Jun 2026. Eight panels collectively count as one idiom.

06 · Heatmap

Compares each state's visitor nights in a quarter with its own five-quarter average (index 1.0). Five points show fluctuations, not established long-run seasonality.

Source: TRA DoTS, visitor nights from Jun 2025 to Jun 2026; each cell is normalised within state.

08 · Dumbbell chart

Shows whether average domestic day-trip spending changed from the June 2025 quarter to June 2026. Spending per trip was derived from regional expenditure divided by trip counts.

Source: TRA DoTS June 2025 / 2026, calculation uses expenditure in $M and day trips in thousands to return AUD per trip. All travel purposes.

10 · Box plot

Shows the spread of average trip length across five quarters in each state; useful for comparing variability, though a five-point distribution is small.

Source: TRA DoTS, quarterly visitor nights ÷ quarterly overnight trips, Jun 2025-Jun 2026.

12 · Grouped bar chart

Compares holiday, visiting friends/relatives, business and other overnight trip reasons. A holiday does not automatically mean visiting a national park.

Source: TRA DoTS, June quarters 2025 and 2026. Counts are estimates by main reason, not nature-specific demand.
