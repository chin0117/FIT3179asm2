CHAPTER 1 / SPATIAL PATTERNS

Where do travel demand and park visits cluster?

Start with the geography. State-level day trips show overall travel demand; the NSW park map shows where nature visits are recorded; the flow map reveals travel connections. Only the park map directly measures visits to natural areas.

01 · Victoria narrowly leads NSW in domestic day-trip volume

The June 2026 quarter shows about 22.47 million trips to Victoria and 22.26 million to NSW. This measures all-purpose day trips, not nature-based participation.

CHAPTER 2 / NATURE-BASED EVIDENCE

What does the nature-specific evidence show?

Now focus on the older, nature-specific series. It reveals changes in visitor segments between 2008 and 2015. The 2024–25 concentration curve describes a separate set of NSW park visits—not a continuation of the time series.

04 · Domestic day visitors became the largest nature segment by 2015

In 2008 domestic overnight nature visitors slightly exceeded domestic day visitors. By 2015 the day segment was larger (19.02m versus 18.14m).

Source: State of the Environment 2016 / Tourism Research Australia, 2008-2015. Million visitor-segment observations.

05 · Domestic day nature visitors grew 48.5% in seven years

Comparing the endpoints makes the growth clear: 12.81 million domestic day nature visitors in 2008 and 19.02 million in 2015. The chart does not describe later years.

Source: State of the Environment 2016 nature visitors dataset.

CHAPTER 3 / DOMESTIC TOURISM CONTEXT

How does broader travel behaviour provide context?

Lastly, examine how domestic overnight trips, visitor nights, spending and trip reasons differ across states in 2025–26. These charts describe all-purpose tourism and must not be treated as measurements of national-park visitation.

08 · State trip volumes peak in different quarters

NSW's highest overnight-trip observation is December 2025, while Victoria's is March 2026. Each mini-chart uses its own vertical scale to highlight changes.

Source: TRA DoTS, Jun 2025 to Jun 2026. Eight panels collectively count as one idiom.

09 · The Northern Territory shows the sharpest relative swings

The NT's visitor-night index ranges from about 0.61 of its five-quarter mean (March 2026) to 1.48 (September 2025). This is only a short observation period.

Source: TRA DoTS, visitor nights from Jun 2025 to Jun 2026; each cell is normalised within state.

10 · Average day-trip spending rose in all eight jurisdictions

In the June quarters compared, NT increased from about $219 to $286 per day trip, while SA increased from $161 to $219. Spending is for all trip purposes.

Source: TRA DoTS June 2025 / 2026, calculation uses expenditure in $M and day trips in thousands to return AUD per trip. All travel purposes.

11 · NT has the widest spread in average trip length

Across five quarters, the NT's average overnight-trip length ranges from about 3.86 to 4.83 nights, a larger spread than the other jurisdictions shown.

Source: TRA DoTS, quarterly visitor nights ÷ quarterly overnight trips, Jun 2025-Jun 2026.

12 · Holiday trips rose while business trips fell

The June 2026 quarter records about 12.95m holiday trips, up from 12.11m in June 2025. Business overnight trips decreased from 3.25m to 2.02m.

Source: TRA DoTS, June quarters 2025 and 2026. Counts are estimates by main reason, not nature-specific demand.

CHAPTER 4 / WHAT WE CAN CONCLUDE

What these patterns mean — and what they cannot tell us

Together, the visualisations tell a focused story: historical nature participation increased, visits to leading NSW parks are unevenly distributed, and wider domestic travel behaviour is variable. These are complementary views, not a single causal explanation.

01

Historic growth

Domestic day nature visitors grew by 48.5% from 2008 to 2015, becoming the largest nature visitor segment by the end of that period.

02

Concentrated visitation

Three NSW parks account for 54.7% of recorded visits within the selected top ten in 2024–25. This does not describe all NSW parks.

03

Different travel patterns

Trip volumes, visitor nights and average spend vary across jurisdictions. These general-tourism figures give context, not direct evidence about park visits.

Sources, definitions and limitations

Different units and periods: Historical nature visitor segments (2008–2015), NSW park visits (2024–25), overnight state-to-state travel (year ending December 2024), and wider domestic tourism (2025–26) are not a continuous time series. The combined historical visitor-segment count is not unique people. Park visits may count the same person more than once.

Maps: The choropleth displays absolute domestic day-trip estimates, not normalised rates; the park map uses approximate locations; and straight lines show state-pair connections rather than physical transport routes.

Data and geographic sources: Tourism Research Australia · Destination NSW park visitation · 2016 State of the Environment · Australian states GeoJSON. Specific dates and measures are stated beneath each chart.
